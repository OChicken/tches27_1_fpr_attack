#+TITLE: SNR

The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), introduced for side-channel analysis by Stefan Mangard (/Hardware Countermeasures against DPA - A Statistical Analysis of Their Effectiveness/, CT-RSA 2004, LNCS 2964, pp. 222-235), is a leakage metric defined at each time sample as the ratio of the between-class variance (signal) to the mean within-class variance (noise). Unlike the t-test or SOST, whose magnitude grows with √n and therefore only measures whether leakage is /detectable/, SNR is independent of the trace count and measures how /exploitable/ the leakage is: it tracks the per-trace separability of the classes (SNR ≈ =d²/4= for a pair), ties directly to template-attack success, and is comparable across experiments. It is used to identify Points of Interest (POI), at which Gaussian templates are subsequently fitted for profiling attacks.

However, under O3 optimisation, instruction interleaving causes the secret-dependent operations to occur at varying time offsets across traces, weakening the within-class distribution and reducing inter-class separability. Consequently, SNR is less effective as a POI selector in this setting. The following analysis is presented as a quantitative characterisation of the leakage rather than as a basis for a practical template attack.
